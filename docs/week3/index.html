What I learned

Describe your design: why these routers, LANs and links, and how you chose the addresses.

I design 4 routers , with 4 lans , and 4 link . The 4 router all connected to 2 other router . This make all router has alternative path if 1 link is down . For addressing i choose address that can be summarized for lan1 and lan2 together and lan3 and lan4 together

Which route summarises several LANs? Which networks does it cover, why is it correct, and what else would it attract if you added a network inside its range?

The route summarizing several LANs is 10.71.0.0/22, which covers the networks 10.71.0.0/23 and 10.71.2.0/23. It is correct because 10.71.0.0/22 range cover 10.71.0.0 until 10.71.3.255. It would attract 10.71.3.0/24 also if this was added to the network because it is in the range of 10.71.0.0/22

When you took a link down, what happened, and why couldn't your routes recover even though another path existed? What would have recovered?

When I took a link down , the packet was dropped and could not reach the destination . This is because the alternative path was not configured in the routing table. The packet would have recovered if the alternative path was added to the routing table . Thus make the packet not dropped and reach the destination .

In your network, what was the control plane? Who worked out the routes, and what does that mean for a network of 40 routers?

The control plane in my network was manually configured. The routes were determined by me based on the network topology and addressing scheme. For a network of 40 routers, this manual approach wuld be impractical and error-prone. I would suggest implementing dynamic routing protocols like OSPF or BGP to automatically route the packet .
